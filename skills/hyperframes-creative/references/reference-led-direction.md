# Reference-led direction and revision

Use this when the user names a reference film/creator, asks for the quality of a prior project, or is choosing between animated directions. Pair it with [generated-media-composition.md](generated-media-composition.md) for material/footage integration and `hyperframes-animation/references/directed-camera.md` for camera implementation. The review-site and release workflow belongs to `hyperframes`; this reference defines what that site should help the director decide.

## Resolve the current brief before inheriting a style

Read the latest user request, the relevant reference, revision-specific selections and current delivery direction. An older production-state heading, original storyboard or selected prototype can be superseded. Record the controlling source, what changed, what is protected, and whether the deliverable is **animated study**, **narration-timed scene**, or **assembled film**. A completed study is not a finished film; approval of a visual language is not a choice of A for every scene.

Describe references in observable terms:

| Axis | Record from actual reference frames / intervals |
| --- | --- |
| Composition | Foreground occluder, midground action, background context; dominant subject and available reading zones |
| Material | Surface scale, edge detail, reflections, contact, depth of field, light direction |
| Temporal design | What changes, where attention moves, how a hold resolves, what receives the next cut |
| Evidence | Which words/numbers are exact; what remains illustrative or conditional |
| Register | Contemporary UI, semantic diagram, material macro, documentary page, human scene; why each appears |

Keep reference observation separate from your interpretation. A black chip example can specify the material finish of a macro shot without ordering an entire film of chips. A preference for light themes does not prohibit dark scenes unless explicitly stated. “Like a technology creator's layered motion breakdown” does not mean “minimal Apple keynote.” Inspect supplied reference media; if its content cannot be accessed, say so and work from the supplied description without claiming to have watched it.

## Prove the direction on representative motion

Before multiplying an unproven treatment across a long film, make a small representative set: a precise mechanism/UI scene, a material/cinematic anchor, and a human/evidence scene when the script needs those registers. These are coverage choices, not a required scene count. Show actual establish → change → resolve animation in the review UI. A strong still, a concept description or a runtime PASS does not prove the animated treatment.

When alternatives are requested, vary the explanatory idea or viewing strategy, not just color or orbit direction. Examples: a persistent IDE handoff route versus a scene of adult collaborators with registered screen content; a same-topology intervention comparison versus a serial incident ledger. Keep the same claim and evidence boundaries so the user can compare them. Unused brainstorms can remain in compact records; their implementation is not mandatory unless requested.

After a benchmark is chosen, write a short **direction card** with accepted frames/intervals, material and type roles, camera grammar, representation mix, unacceptable failures and protected elements. Scale that grammar to the film, with scene-specific mechanisms. Recheck representative motion after a style migration: changing cream to graphite does not modernize an antique rotor metaphor.

For a rejected material hero, diagnose three scales before adding detail: **dominant silhouette → functional assemblies → surface finish**. The early machine-audits-machine build lacked the middle scale and a visible inspection consequence; a textured background and many tiny bolts could not repair that. Improve composition/contact/action first, then material. This is a method to diagnose an intended physical hero, not a requirement that a simple UI explanation contain machinery.

## Assign a representation to the claim

An earlier successful film direction combined the following; use it as a starting vocabulary when that project is the requested benchmark, not as a universal palette or quota.

| Claim needs | Useful representation | What must remain exact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical scale, atmosphere, an entry into a world | Generated silicon macro, optical layers, city/port footage | Fiction/example qualifier and factual overlays |
| Code changes, review, outputs, evidence | Contemporary IDE, observability workspace, document or chart | Realistic code, legible hierarchy, file/line correspondence, numbers and labels |
| Causal sequence or repeated dependency | One finite SVG/DOM/canvas world with readable return states | Topology, event order, conditional edges, shared landmarks |
| Human judgment, handoff, deferred repair | Authored adult character/physical scene, selective generated performance | Exact monitor contents, mechanism, uncertainty; no implied documentary authenticity |
| A conceptual contrast | Large type, aligned comparison, simple semantic geometry | The contrast itself, readable scale and adequate dwell |
| Depth changes the explanation | Authored 3D specimen or layered 2.5D | Visual admission gate, world coherence and deterministic motion |

Plain diagrams, cards, circles and big text remain valid when they make the idea clearer. “Avoid slideshow” addresses a sequence without meaningful development; it is not a ban on a requested large-text comparison. Conversely, a rich material world must leave enough contrast and scale for its exact overlay. If a user says the overlay is too weak, inspect the composite before generating more detail behind it.

## Convert comments into bounded changes

Keep the review choice and the change request as separate facts. Apply the project's declared choice semantics: in one earlier film, A/B/both without a note meant approval of the corresponding version; neither meant rejection; no entry meant unreviewed. New projects should preserve their own interface semantics. Agent QA must never create user approvals.

Classify each current shot:

- **PROTECT:** Preserve the accepted concept, material and explicitly named interval. A protected opening can coexist with a rebuilt remainder.
- **POLISH:** Repair the named issue while keeping the accepted concept: alignment, scale, contrast, registration, timing, transition or incomplete finish.
- **REBUILD:** Replace a rejected/failed mechanism; reuse approved material only if it still serves the new idea.
- **UNREVIEWED:** Continue under the user's existing direction and discretion; do not claim approval.

Record a repair as `revision + shot/interval + observed problem + requested result + changed owner + verification frame/window`. Examples from actual notes:

| Comment | Concrete acceptance test |
| --- | --- |
| “The old calculation reached the right edge; this version ends early.” | The complete calculation reaches its final output before the cut; protected upstream states survive. |
| “Inside / on the circle / outside.” | Three clearly different human-to-boundary relationships share a comparable frame; outside figure does not collide with title. |
| “Concept works; layout/alignment has problems.” | Keep the mechanism, align comparison anchors and inspect every camera pose, especially final overview. |
| “Original and compressed code aren't different enough.” | Show visible naming/spacing/character differences and the code-owned condensed form; preserve any stated behavior/test constraint. |
| “Use big text like PPT here.” | Build the requested readable contrast; remove unnecessary arrows/circles instead of adding cinematic ornament. |
| “Opening 0–6 seconds is good; rebuild after that.” | Preserve the named footage interval and identity; inspect the new handoff for old layers leaking through. |

If a later review contradicts an earlier preference, update the active direction and protect only the parts still approved. Do not erase the old decision: record its superseding revision so a later assembly cannot silently revert to it.

## Review the argument at the current clock

Compare the reference, approved candidate and actual encoded scene at matched semantic moments. A 12-second silent proposal may become a substantially longer narrated scene. Re-author its internal cue windows; proportional stretching can reveal answers early, suppress the last state or leave a dead tail. Material video has its own source clock; see the media-composition reference.

For each shot, inspect the establishing state, every claim-bearing change, return/synthesis and outgoing edge. Add denser samples to long holds and exact before/after frames around findings. For seams, inspect the outgoing focus, incoming receiver, caption rail and sound plan together. A beautiful middle frame does not excuse a blank first second, hidden final qualification or unchanged 26-second tail.

Record observation, proposed repair and verified outcome separately. A source fix is **pending encoded confirmation** until checked in the new encode. Preserve a strong scene if the alleged defect is an intentional readable hold; the proof is that the hold still performs the spoken synthesis, not that every second moves. Keep visual observations, source analysis, technical checks and listening claims distinct.

## Evidence and limits

This reference is self-contained; private project files and chat histories are not part of the release. In your project retain brief revisions, candidate manifests, versioned choices, protected intervals and audit confirmations. Prefer the current output's evidence: a historical plan, earlier acceptance or partial contact sheet is not fresh full-film verification.
